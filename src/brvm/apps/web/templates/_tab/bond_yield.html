{{ "Yield & risk glossary"|t }} — {{ "formulas + reading guide"|t }}
{{ "Clean price"|t }} Quoted market price, excluding accrued interest since the last coupon. What you see on brvm.org's cotation page.
{{ "Accrued coupon"|t }} Interest that has built up since the last coupon date. Belongs to the seller in a settled trade.
{{ "Dirty price"|t }} Clean + accrued — the actual cash paid on settlement. YTM discounts future cash flows to the dirty price, not the clean.
{{ "Current yield"|t }} Annual coupon / clean price. Quick income yield if you hold for a year; ignores capital gain/loss to maturity, so trades above par read too high and trades below par too low.
{{ "Yield to maturity"|t }} The single yield that discounts all remaining coupons + principal to the dirty price. Total-return yield if you hold to maturity and reinvest coupons at the same rate. Solved by bisection over [−50%, +100%]. Returns "—" for prices outside that bracket.
{{ "Macaulay duration"|t }} Weighted-average time to receive the bond's cash flows, in years, weighted by each cash flow's present value. Always < maturity for coupon-paying bonds (interim coupons pull the average earlier).
{{ "Modified duration"|t }} Macaulay / (1 + y). Approximate percent price change per 1 percentage point parallel shift in yield: ΔP/P ≈ −ModD × Δy. A 5-year modified duration means a +1% yield bump implies roughly a −5% price move.
{{ "Convexity"|t }} Second-order correction: ΔP/P ≈ −ModD × Δy + ½ × Convexity × Δy². Always positive for vanilla bonds — captures the fact that duration under-predicts price gains when yields fall and over-predicts losses when yields rise. Higher = more curvature = the linear duration approximation errs more on large yield moves.
